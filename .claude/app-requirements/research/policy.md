# Research: policy

> Snapshot from 2026-10-14, written by Claude's research agents for the app spec (`../CLAUDE.md`). Each finding lists its sources and a confidence level. Recheck anything time-sensitive (versions, prices, store policies) before acting on it.

## Summary
TLDR: an Apple app transfer only works for an app that already has a version released on the App Store. So "Thomas's Individual account now, move it to the church before public launch" can't be done with Apple's transfer tool. The church's Organization team should own the production bundle ID (the app's permanent unique identifier) before the first TestFlight upload, because once a build is uploaded that ID can never be reused. Costs (verified): Apple is $99/yr per account, and the fee is waived for recognized nonprofits that offer only free apps. Organization accounts need a free D-U-N-S number (a business ID from Dun & Bradstreet; up to 5 business days, plus 2 days before Apple can see it). One risk: Apple wants the nonprofit "officially recognized by a relevant authority", and many churches don't have an IRS determination letter. Google Play is a one-time $25. A new personal Play account must verify a physical Android device and run a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days in a row. Thomas has no Android device, so the church's Organization Play account is the better route; org accounts are widely reported as exempt from both rules (medium confidence). The simplest compliant donation path is a "Give" button that opens the church's existing giving page in Safari: Apple 3.2.2(iv) allows collecting charity funds "outside of the app, such as via Safari", and Google Play bars its own billing for "tax exempt donations". Stripe, Apple Pay and Candid are not needed in v1. The PWYW (pay what you want) or "tip the developer" framing must be avoided, because that falls under in-app purchase rules. Privacy: on-device speech recognition is "not collected" on both stores. But iOS only honors on-device mode when the device supports it, so the app must disable speech recognition rather than fall back to server recognition. Accounts mean email, user ID and synced study data must be disclosed. Both stores require account deletion that can be started in the app, plus a privacy policy linked in the app and in the store listing. New 2026 items: Texas SB2420 has been in force since June 4, 2026 (it matters if the church beta is in Texas), and the expo-age-range package handles the age APIs. Apple's age-rating questions became mandatory in September 2026, and YouVersion's Bible app is rated 13+ for Scripture content. Builds need the Xcode 26 / iOS 26 SDK (since Apr 28, 2026), and Play requires target API 36 (since Aug 31, 2026).

## Findings
- **[high]** Apple Developer Program costs 99 USD per membership year for both Individual and Organization. An Individual account shows the person's legal name as the App Store seller; an Organization account shows the organization's name.
  - Individual: legal name, address (no P.O. boxes), 2FA Apple Account. Organization: must be a legal entity that can sign contracts (DBAs, trade names and branches are not accepted), a D-U-N-S Number (except government), a public website and an email on the org's domain. The enroller must have legal binding authority: owner, executive, senior project lead, or an employee given authority by a senior employee. In practice a pastor or church administrator enrolls as Account Holder and invites Thomas as Admin.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/
- **[high]** D-U-N-S is free. Allow up to 5 business days for issuance (expediting does not shorten this), then up to 2 business days for Apple to receive it. Google says it can take up to 30 days.
  - Check Apple's D-U-N-S lookup tool first; the church may already have a number. The name must exactly match the legal entity name.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/D-U-N-S/, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312
- **[high]** Apple's fee waiver covers legal entities with nonprofit status 'officially recognized by a relevant authority' (the IRS in the US) that distribute only free apps and sell no digital goods. Individuals and sole proprietors are excluded.
  - Request it during enrollment, or later from the Account Holder via the contact form. The church confirms eligibility at each renewal. If the app ever sells IAP the waiver ends and the 99 USD fee applies. Religious organizations are not mentioned. Gotcha: churches are not legally required to obtain an IRS determination letter. A 2023 forum post from a church reports a stalled waiver for exactly this reason (low confidence: https://origin-devforums.apple.com/forums/profile/RevelationChurchMarion/comments). Have a determination letter or a denominational group-exemption letter ready.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/support/membership-fee-waiver/
- **[high]** Converting Thomas's Individual Apple membership into the church's Organization membership is limited to the organization's 'founder/cofounder', so it is not a realistic path.
  - Account Holder transfers for individual memberships are 'granted on a limited basis' and need Apple's help.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/updating-your-account-information/
- **[high]** Apple app transfer requires that 'The app must have at least one version that was released to the App Store.' It also requires both accounts to have accepted the latest agreements and no pending review or processing status.
  - A TestFlight-only app cannot be transferred. This contradicts the plan to transfer before public launch. Either the church team owns the production app record from the first TestFlight upload, or v1 ships publicly under Thomas's name and is transferred afterward.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/app-transfer-criteria
- **[high]** Once any build has been uploaded, its bundle ID can never be reused, even after the app record is removed. Removed apps also lose their name reservation and SKU.
  - Pick the production bundle ID (e.g. org.<church>.seedling) only inside the church's team. Use a separate dev ID (e.g. com.<thomas>.seedling.dev) under Thomas's Individual team for personal builds. Changing bundle ID means a new app: beta testers reinstall, and the on-device SQLite data does not carry over. Plan an export/import or server-backup restore.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/create-an-app-record/remove-an-app
- **[high]** What an Apple app transfer carries over: App ID/bundle ID, reviews and ratings, iCloud containers, and the Sign in with Apple Services ID (unless disassociated first). Before starting, TestFlight must be turned off by removing all builds and testers.
  - What does not carry over or must be redone: the Apple Pay merchant ID, the APNs key/cert (recipient uses its own), new promo codes, Xcode Cloud data (must be deleted first), and pre-transfer sales/analytics. The recipient regenerates the shared secret. IAP product IDs must not collide with IDs already in the recipient's account.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/overview-of-app-transfer
- **[high]** Sign in with Apple user IDs belong to one team. After a transfer they must be migrated within 60 days using TN3159.
  - Steps: (1) Before transferring, the old team gets a client_credentials token with scope=user.migration from https://appleid.apple.com/auth/token. (2) It generates a transfer_sub per user at https://appleid.apple.com/auth/usermigrationinfo (needs the new team's team ID). (3) It ungroups apps and initiates the transfer. (4) The new team exchanges each transfer_sub for its new team-scoped sub (plus private relay email) with its own client secret. Missing the 60 days means transferring the app back and forth again. Firebase Auth stores the Apple sub as the provider UID, so existing Firebase-Apple accounts break unless remapped. This is an inference; no Firebase doc covers it.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3159-migrating-sign-in-with-apple-users-for-an-app-transfer
- **[high]** Google Play charges a one-time US$25 registration fee for both personal and organization accounts. Organization accounts need a D-U-N-S number, organization website and phone. Google says organizations including nonprofits should use the organization type.
  - What Play shows publicly: personal accounts show legal name, country and developer email (full address if monetizing). Organization accounts show legal name, legal address, developer email and phone. Legal name and address must be verified before publishing, and payment methods may need a deposit challenge. Prepaid cards are not accepted, and the fee is not refunded if verification fails.
  - sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13634885, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312
- **[high]** Personal Play accounts created after Nov 13, 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 consecutive days, then 'Apply for production'. Google usually reviews in about 7 days.
  - N is 12 (third-party sources say it dropped from 20 in Dec 2024). Organization accounts are not mentioned on Google's page; third-party guides consistently say they are exempt (medium confidence). A tester who opts out restarts the clock.
  - sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465
- **[high]** New personal Play accounts must also verify access to a physical, non-rooted Android 10+ phone through the Play Console mobile app before publishing.
  - Thomas has only an iPhone, so a personal Play account means borrowing or buying an Android phone. The page speaks only of personal accounts (org exemption is medium confidence).
  - sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14316361
- **[high]** Google Play app transfer moves users, ratings, reviews, stats and policy declarations. It does not move test tracks (closed-test testers must re-opt-in), linked services (Firebase must be relinked) or pre-transfer orders. Google answers within about 2 business days.
  - You need the target account's registration-fee transaction ID, and both accounts must be complete and active. Unlike Apple, the page states no 'must be released' rule (inference: draft and testing apps can be transferred).
  - sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247
- **[high]** Android developer verification: Play developers using Play App Signing get their apps registered automatically. Enforcement on certified devices began Sept 30, 2026 in some regions, with a global rollout in 2027.
  - Mainly matters for sideloading. Keep Play App Signing (EAS default) and don't lose the upload key.
  - sources: https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq
- **[high]** Apple 3.2.1(vi): 'Approved nonprofits may fundraise directly within their own apps or third-party apps' if campaigns follow all guidelines and support Apple Pay. 3.2.2(iv): everyone else must keep the app free and 'may only collect funds outside of the app, such as via Safari or SMS.'
  - Separately, 3.1.1 says tipping the developer or content providers must use IAP. Framing matters: 'Give to <Church> for the Great Commission' is a charitable donation; 'support the developer' or PWYW is an IAP case. Open the page with React Native Linking.openURL, which launches Safari, not an in-app browser (SFSafariViewController), to match 'via Safari' (inference).
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/
- **[high]** A US nonprofit becomes Apple-approved for in-app Apple Pay donations by holding any Candid Seal of Transparency. All seal holders are approved, the seal is free, and Apple charges no fee or commission. Non-US nonprofits apply through Benevity using their Team ID.
  - Bronze needs contact info, a donation mailing address, mission and leadership. A church not in the IRS Business Master File (the IRS list of exempt organizations) must send IRS documents (e.g. EIN letter, determination letter, group-exemption proof) to get listed (getlisted@candid.org). Also needs a payment provider and an Apple Pay implementation. Not worth it for v1.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/nonprofits/, https://help.candid.org/s/article/How-to-Earn-a-Seal-of-Transparency, https://help.candid.org/s/article/How-to-Get-Your-Nonprofit-Listed-on-GuideStar
- **[medium]** US storefront: since the May 1, 2025 update after Epic v. Apple, apps may include buttons, external links and calls to action to other purchase methods without an entitlement. The Ninth Circuit (Dec 2025) upheld contempt but vacated the zero-commission ban and sent the rate back to the district court. Apple proposed tiered rates (reported 15%/10%/5%) in Aug 2026; no rate set as of the latest reports, so the effective US rate is still 0.
  - Mostly irrelevant here: a charitable donation to a third-party 501(c)(3) is governed by 3.2.2(iv) in every storefront, not by the digital-goods link-out rules (inference). The Supreme Court denied Apple's stay on May 6, 2026; the contempt question is pending.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=9txfddzf, https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/, https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/ninth-circuit-largely-upholds-ruling-in-epic-v-apple, https://www.macobserver.com/news/apple-epic-link-out-commission-proposal-15-percent/, https://www.courthousenews.com/apples-fight-over-commissions-for-linked-out-app-store-purchases-continues-in-district-court/
- **[high]** Google Play Payments policy lists 'tax exempt donations' among payments that must NOT use Google Play's billing system.
  - Linking out to the church's 501(c)(3) giving page is fine on Android. Donations to Thomas personally are not tax-exempt, so this exemption would not apply.
  - sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738
- **[medium]** Stripe's standard US online card rate is 2.9% + 30¢. A nonprofit discount exists for registered nonprofits with at least 80% of Stripe volume from tax-deductible donations (request it through Stripe support). Third parties put the rate at 2.2% + 30¢; Stripe's own pages do not state it.
  - Standard rate and eligibility: high confidence. The 2.2% + 30¢ figure: unverified on stripe.com. The vision doc's plan (Stripe + a separate Chase account in Thomas's name) would make gifts personal, non-deductible income and push Apple toward the IAP tip rules. Use the church's existing giving processor instead.
  - sources: https://stripe.com/pricing, https://support.stripe.com/questions/fee-discount-for-nonprofit-organizations, https://wiredimpact.com/blog/stripe-for-nonprofits/
- **[high]** Apple privacy labels: 'collect' means sending data off the device and keeping it longer than needed to answer the request in real time. 'Data that is processed only on device is not collected.' Apple is responsible for data Apple itself collects; developers disclose third-party SDK data.
  - Likely answers for Seedling with optional accounts and backup/sync. Contact Info > Email Address, Identifiers > User ID, User Content > Other User Content (synced decks, journeys, group content), and probably Usage Data > Product Interaction (synced FSRS review history, i.e. the spaced-repetition scheduler's records). All Linked to User, purpose App Functionality. Name only if a display name is stored. Diagnostics (Crash/Performance) only if a crash SDK is added. Audio Data: not collected, as long as recognition is truly on-device. Tracking: No.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/
- **[high]** Google Play Data safety: 'collect' means sent off the device, including by SDKs; on-device-only processing is exempt. The form asks about encryption in transit and a deletion mechanism. A privacy policy is required even for apps that collect nothing.
  - Mirror the Apple answers. Personal info: Email address and User IDs (optional, since accounts are optional). App activity: App interactions and/or Other user-generated content (purpose App functionality, not shared). Audio > Voice or sound recordings: not collected. Encrypted in transit: Yes. Deletion: Yes. Firebase Auth itself collects IP addresses (abuse prevention), user agent and the Firebase App ID; Crashlytics adds crash logs and an installation UUID.
  - sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469, https://firebase.google.com/docs/android/play-data-disclosure
- **[high]** Apple 5.1.1(v): apps that support account creation must let users start full account deletion inside the app, not just deactivation. It can hand off to a direct web link. Non-regulated apps may not require email, phone or chat to delete. Sign in with Apple tokens must be revoked through the REST API.
  - Automatically created guest accounts are covered too, so avoid Firebase anonymous auth for local-only users. With Firebase JS SDK + Apple: reauthenticate, then revokeAccessToken(auth, token), then deleteUser (https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/web/apple). Also delete the user's Postgres rows and tell users the timeline.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/, https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/
- **[high]** Google Play: apps with in-app account creation need an in-app deletion path and a working web deletion link entered in Play Console. The web link must name the app or developer as listed and must not force reinstalling the app. Deletion covers all data declared in Data safety, and any retention must be disclosed.
  - One web page (e.g. on the church site) plus the in-app flow satisfies both stores.
  - sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111
- **[high]** Privacy policy: Apple requires a link in App Store Connect metadata and inside the app. Google requires the Play Console field plus in-app access, on an 'active, publicly accessible and non-geofenced URL (no PDFs)' titled as a privacy policy.
  - Google's required contents: developer info and privacy contact, data accessed/collected/used/shared and with whom, secure handling, and retention/deletion policy. The entity named in the store listing must appear in the policy. Terms of use: Apple's Standard EULA applies if none is supplied (https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/provide-a-custom-license-agreement/). Write custom Terms/Community Guidelines anyway if Groups expose user content.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311
- **[high]** Apple 1.2: apps with user-generated content need a filter for objectionable material, a way to report it with timely response, user blocking and published contact info. A June 2026 paragraph puts removal responsibility on the developer.
  - Applies if Groups or shared Journeys show user-authored content to others.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/, https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=a233fmpw
- **[high]** Apple privacy manifests in Expo: with Continuous Native Generation (Expo's prebuild workflow), declare required-reason APIs under expo.ios.privacyManifests in app.json (e.g. NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryUserDefaults with reason CA92.1). Expo SDK packages ship their own PrivacyInfo, but static CocoaPods third-party manifests may not be read, so copy their reasons in.
  - Common categories: UserDefaults, FileTimestamp, SystemBootTime, DiskSpace, ActiveKeyboards. Check node_modules/<pkg>/ios/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy for each native dependency (SQLite driver, Firebase, speech recognition). Apple emails about missing reasons within minutes of a TestFlight upload. Current Expo SDK is 57 per the 'latest' docs.

- **[high]** Amended COPPA Rule: effective June 23, 2025, general compliance date April 22, 2026. Changes include separate parental consent before third-party disclosures such as targeted ads, retention limits, a written security program, a new 'mixed audience' definition, and biometric identifiers (including voiceprints) added to 'personal information'.
  - Voiceprints listed: medium confidence (law-firm quote of the rule text). Proposed 'data derived from voice' language was dropped.
  - sources: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-register-notices/16-cfr-part-312-coppa-final-rule-amendments, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule, https://hunton.sitepilot11.firmseek.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-publishes-final-coppa-rule-amendments
- **[high]** FTC COPPA FAQ: ask age 'in a neutral manner'. Don't default to 13+, don't say under-13 can't participate, an 'I am over 12' checkbox is not neutral, and use a cookie or local flag to stop back-button re-entry. Data on the device that is 'never transmitted' is not collected. Persistent identifiers are personal information unless used only for internal operations.
  - FAQ D.7, H.3, D.4/D.6 (mixed audience: you may age-screen but not block under-13s; offer age-appropriate features), F.5, C.5/F.2. For the 13+ account gate: ask birth month/year neutrally at account creation. Under-13 users get local-only mode with no network calls, no analytics and no crash IDs. Avoid child-appealing store imagery so the app isn't treated as child-directed.
  - sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions
- **[medium]** Texas SB2420 (App Store Accountability Act) has been enforceable since about June 4, 2026: the Fifth Circuit stayed the injunction and the Supreme Court declined to intervene on July 6, 2026. Apple applies it to new Texas Apple Accounts; developers use the Declared Age Range API and the Significant Change API (PermissionKit). Google's Play Age Signals API serves Texas accounts created after May 28, 2026.
  - Developer duties: assign age ratings, use store age and consent signals for minors, flag 'significant changes' (an age-rating change counts), and handle consent revocation. Merits appeal pending (CCIA v. Paxton, No. 25-51073); nothing found after July 2026. Utah delayed to May 6, 2027 (HB 498, private enforcement only); Louisiana to July 1, 2027 (HB 977); California AB 1043 starts Jan 1, 2027 (developers request an OS/store age signal). The first-party Expo package is expo-age-range (iOS Declared Age Range + Android Play Age Signals). It needs Xcode 26+ and the com.apple.developer.declared-age-range entitlement (https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/age-range/). Get legal review.
  - sources: https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=2ezb6jhj, https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2026/7/7/supreme-court-clears-the-way-texass-app-store-accountability-act-is-now-enforceable, https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/, https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/use-age-signals-api, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691
- **[high]** Apple age ratings are now 4+/9+/13+/16+/18+ and come from a questionnaire (content, in-app controls, capabilities like UGC, messaging, social media, web access). ## recommendations
- 1. Start the church's paperwork now, since it has the longest lead time. Confirm the church is an incorporated legal entity with an EIN and has an IRS determination letter or group-exemption letter. Name the authorized signer. Get the D-U-N-S (5+2 business days for Apple, up to 30 days per Google).
- 2. Make the church's Apple Organization team own the production bundle ID (e.g. org.<church>.seedling) before the first TestFlight upload. Use Thomas's $99 Individual account, or a 7-day free Personal Team, only for Phase 0 personal builds under a separate dev bundle ID. This avoids the 'must be released before transfer' rule and Sign in with Apple migration entirely.
- 3. Skip a personal Google Play account. Open the church's Organization Play Console ($25) when the Android beta starts and add Thomas as a user. This avoids Android device verification and the 12-tester/14-day gate (exemption is medium confidence). Still borrow an Android phone for real-device QA before public launch.
- 4. Donations v1: a 'Give' button that calls Linking.openURL(<church giving URL>) and opens Safari or the default browser. Copy: a gift to <Church> (501(c)(3)) for the Great Commission. ## gotchas
- Apple app transfer needs at least one released App Store version. A TestFlight-only app cannot be transferred, so 'transfer before public launch' is impossible with Apple's tool.
- Once any build is uploaded, that bundle ID is burned for good. Changing bundle ID later means a new app: testers reinstall and on-device SQLite study data is lost unless backed up or exported.
- Sign in with Apple user IDs are team-scoped. After a transfer you have 60 days to run the TN3159 migration, and Firebase accounts linked to Apple would need remapping.
- Turning TestFlight off (removing all builds and testers) is a precondition for an Apple transfer. Google transfers drop all test tracks, so testers must re-opt-in.
- Church fee waiver: Apple wants nonprofit status 'officially recognized' (IRS). Many churches never got a determination letter, and a church reported a stalled waiver for this reason.
- Only someone with legal authority (pastor or admin) can enroll the church with Apple, not a volunteer developer. Apple may phone them to verify. An org-domain email and a live website are required.
- A personal Google Play account needs a physical Android 10+ phone for device verification, plus 12 testers opted in 14 consecutive days. Thomas has only an iPhone.
- Personal accounts publicly display Thomas's legal name: the Apple seller name, and the Google legal name and country (full address if monetizing).
- Framing donations as PWYW or 'support the developer' turns them into Apple IAP tips. Donations to Thomas personally aren't 'tax exempt', so Google's billing exemption doesn't cover them.
- Open the giving page in Safari (Linking.openURL), not an in-app WebView or SFSafariViewController, to match 3.2.2(iv)'s 'outside of the app, such as via Safari' (inference).
- Firebase anonymous auth creates 'guest accounts' that Apple says must be deletable, and it starts data collection. Don't use it for local-only users.
- iOS requiresOnDeviceRecognition is honored only when supportsOnDeviceRecognition is true, and the Expo library enables it 'only if the device supports it'. A silent server fallback would break the 'audio never leaves device' promise.
- Scripture content can raise the age rating: YouVersion's Bible is 13+ for infrequent violence, mature themes and alcohol references. A rating change later counts as a 'significant change' under Texas SB2420.
- Texas SB2420 is in force (since ~June 4, 2026), and the church beta is probably in Texas. Plan Declared Age Range / Play Age Signals handling, and get counsel, before teen accounts exist there.
- Since September 2026 the age-rating questionnaire, including social-media questions, is required on every new submission or update.
- Firebase Auth collects IP addresses and user agent for abuse prevention, and Cloud Run request logs also capture IPs. Mention these in the privacy policy even though no Apple label category maps directly to IP.
- Google rejects PDF privacy policies. Both stores require the policy link inside the app as well as in the store listing.
- EAS ad hoc builds are capped at 100 iPhones per year, and disabled devices still count. Use TestFlight for the church beta.
- Builds must use Xcode 26 / the iOS 26 SDK (since Apr 28, 2026) and target Android API 36 (since Aug 31, 2026, extension to Nov 1, 2026).
